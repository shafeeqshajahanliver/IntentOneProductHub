                      A note on sizing, timings and contingency

                      These are forecasts, not commitments. The method is new, built in the two weeks after Bash Week, and the caveats below are as important as the dates.

                      How the sizes were produced

                      1. A baseline from Linear. Completed work, weighted towards the projects logged during Bash Week, where tickets carried far more detail than older ones.
                      2. A sizing skill. Albert and Javier built a Claude skill, three data files and a prompt, that runs over a whole project and sizes every issue. It marks each one estimatable or not and names what is missing: no acceptance criteria, no success definition, a thin description. It rates complexity by ticket fidelity, dependencies, feature size and how much of the codebase is touched, then compares against historical work of similar complexity.
                      3. A human pass. A short sizing session per pod with the engineering lead, product facilitating, Albert present. The skill is the first pass that finds the gaps, not the answer.
                      4. A Bash Week adjustment. The historical data predates Bash Week, so raw output runs long. Albert reviews and cuts. Both numbers sit side by side in a sheet, one tab per pod: the raw estimate and the adjusted one.
                      5. Into the roadmap. Sized initiatives carry their estimate in the detail view, linked to that sheet. Milestone dates are then paced so the client bands beneath them still hold.

                      What that means for the dates

                      • The cut is judgement, not a measured multiplier. Nobody has a velocity figure from Bash Week yet. The adjustment is one experienced view of how much faster the new way of working is.
                      • Estimate quality tracks ticket quality. Where a ticket is thin the skill says so rather than guessing. Those items are the least reliable on the board.
                      • August capacity is not modelled. Most of engineering is in Spain, where August is holiday season. The skill counts working days without knowing who is away.
                      • Sequence is firmer than any single date. Milestones are anchored to what a client can do, not to a calendar promise. If a date moves, the order of the trenches is what holds.
                      • Q4-Q1 carries the contingency. Anything without a date sits there on purpose, and sits in Linear as Later with no date and no size.

                      Where the Intelligence column comes from

                      • The Intelligence sequence follows the IntentAI three month plan for August to October: publish the baseline, fix the signal, prove the improvement in the product, then encode and model. Each step is the evidence for the next, so the order is firmer than the dates.
                      • The SDK proof point carries a people dependency rather than an engineering one. If the SDK allocation is not confirmed, the documented fallback is that the improvement closes on weblogs first with the SDK following, and the 16 Oct entry moves.

                      Estimates as at 10 Aug 2026. Expect them to move as tickets get written up and the next round of sizing lands.

                          IE vs IntentOne · the proof

                          This is what the IntentOne testing milestone on the chart above actually is. The client keeps working the way they work today, and we run the same briefs three ways alongside them. No track touches activation: every campaign is recreated in the client's own campaign management system, so the race isolates the one thing we claim is better, how audiences get made. Nothing is switched off until the testing window has answered that question.

                          Step one · before any race

                          The data has to agree first

                          An automated QA health report runs on the IntentOne base data for that client and compares it against what IE produces on the same base: counts, coverage, signal types and the enrichment behind them. The Data Audit is the evidence, and it runs on a schedule rather than as a one-off check. If the two do not agree, we fix that before anyone builds an audience, because an A/B test on data the client does not trust proves nothing.
