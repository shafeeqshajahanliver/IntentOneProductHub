Product Definition

Intent HQ is the Human Context Layer — the read of real customer lives that feeds every system a business already runs. IntentOne is the Opportunity Intelligence product built on it: it encodes on-device and cloud signals as sequences of need, and activates the moment a customer is ready to act.

Part one · What we sell

We don't sit on top of your stack.
We feed it.

The category is the Human Context Layer: one continuously refreshed read of every customer — who they are, what's forming, and the moment of need — fed into every tool the business already runs. We're not a CDP and not a copilot. We're the brains; your stack is the hands.

The shift we're selling Coda ↗

Customers are starting to delegate decisions to AI agents — "find me a better deal, sort my renewal." The agent does the comparing; the customer accepts the recommendation. Once delegation happens, your messaging, timing and offers no longer reach them. You're missing opportunities every day, losing revenue — the moment of need was visible weeks before she asked, in signal your tools never see. Brands that wait for customers to decide will lose to brands that knew it was coming.

The consequence for how we sell: IntentOne is a P&L conversation, not a martech conversation. The deck opens on a customer-value waterfall (new adds + upsell + win-back − churn avoided), not on features. Every opportunity is a unit of value with a forecasted ROI and a measured outcome over a holdout.

Propensity says who. Persuadability says who's worth acting on.

The mandated core differentiator frame. Two predictions decide where to act: propensity (the moment of need) and persuadability (causal uplift). The second is where the value lives — predicting who acts anyway earns little. The simulator rehearses every candidate action through the world model — P(outcome | do(action)) — taught by a causal AI teacher (CATE, learned from randomised holdouts). Only Persuadables earn an impression.

01 · The Edge SDK Coda ↗

Zero-party data, minted on the device. Most AI consumes data that already exists; the Edge SDK generates net-new, consented signal — 50 sensor signals turned into 16,000 data points and 750 insights, computed where behaviour actually happens, under 1% battery. The intelligence comes to the data, not the data to the intelligence: raw signals never leave the phone, only aggregated enrichments do.

Two minds

Fast mind, slow mind

The edge is the fast mind — micro-intents classified every ~5 seconds, activation in the same on-device runtime, no round-trip, no missed moment. The cloud is the slow mind — roughly a trillion actions a day read at full granularity, finding the sequences that recur across millions of lives.

Defensibility

Nothing to copy from

~300,000 lines of expert code with no open equivalent — nothing for LLMs to learn from, nothing for others to copy. A competitor needs three multi-year programmes at once: the persistent on-device runtime, the network-scale foundation, and a privacy architecture that survives audit.

Beyond marketing

One SDK, many verticals

Cyber, insurance, health, identity — the deck explicitly claims value far beyond marketing. It enriches every dimension of behaviour (when, where, what, who), sharpening rich first-party data and standing in where it's thin. Standing rule: Edge SDK data is always one of the recommended data sources — it's our IP.

Where the SDK is going — behavioural spans/tokens (IntentAI SDK planning, 21 Aug): per-user, time-bounded tokens across when / where / what / who, shipping via a dedicated behavioural reports channel; a dedicated Edge engineer embeds with the AI team; the enrichment roadmap moves to the AI team. Naming: internal decision renames "microsegments" → "enrichments" — but deck v4 still says "micro-segments" twice. Flagged for the Message Maps.

What a client receives

The canonical objects

A Customer DNA Profile per person plus the enrichment families — Interests & Topics (BTI), Brand Affinities, intents at three horizons (micro ~5-sec / macro weekly / long-term monthly), 85+ evidence-backed behavioural segments, Moments of Need (four types, each with a score and an expiry), and a 384-dimensional embedding per user — all on one stitched identity (12% documented AUC uplift).

Why it's cheap

Lowest cost per prediction

Global pattern library: write once on GPU, match on CPU forever — patterns cost compute once, then serve millions. Edge inference avoids egress; batch inference is amortised; embeddings beat rules (50× faster cohort builds); <80ms profile reads. Custom models, not LLMs, are the classification path at scale (~1 token vs ~100 — LLM-only was too expensive).

Now in build

Baseline → 80% → 90%

A quality baseline for brands / topics / interests on MTN Nigeria and Verizon data is the benchmark for all improvement (running ~1 week late as of 24 Aug); IntentAI is extending to SDK data for BTI extraction for Edge customers (Vivo, Fishka). Three things an IntentAI conversation must never do: sell the engine as a database (the moment it becomes "yet another customer table" the differentiation collapses); skip the learning loop (every deployment contracts for outcome feedback); or promise headless-only consumption today. Deployment modes — where the line is drawn Coda ↗

One intelligence chain; the deployment mode decides where the vendor/customer boundary sits. "We change where the line is drawn, not what we build." All modes share one Actuator framework, so customers progress modes without re-integrating — the actuators are the same, the boundary moves. This is the operational truth behind "we feed your stack".

Mode A · End-to-end (embedded) — Blue Box friendly

The client onboards the full spine and uses the IntentOne UI end-to-end. Actuators: JEMA for push; Twilio-class provider for RCS / WhatsApp / SMS. Default for mid-market without mature martech, greenfield programmes, single-vendor buyers. Days to first signal. Wrong when the client is invested in an enterprise activation stack — "asking them to use our UI will cost the deal."

Mode B · Composed (hybrid) — most enterprise deals land here

The client keeps their activation stack; IntentOne plugs in at one of four exits. "Replacing their stack is not the sale. Making it perform better is."

  • B1 · Enrichment (exit at Data): enrichment families + Customer DNA Profile with embeddings, reverse-ETL'd to warehouse/CDP. Fastest to land. Priced on records enriched.
  • B2 · Decisions (exit at Opportunities) — the commercial wedge: ranked, evidenced, time-bound opportunities with predicted ROI plus audience expansion. Highest price per unit. (Frame expansions as Persuadables, not lookalikes — see watchlist.)
  • B3 · Personalisation (exit at Message): per-person recommendations delivered by the client's stack.
  • B4 · Campaign handoff (exit at Activation): one campaign-ready package ingested natively by SFMC, Adobe Journey Optimizer, Braze or in-house. "Make our CMS smarter, not replace it."

Three Mode B don'ts: don't let the first exit become the whole story (B1 is a foothold); don't lose the learning loop (no outcome feedback = a feature sale, not a product sale); don't quietly replatform.

Mode C · Headless — the destination, not a 2026 commitment

Opportunity engine and personalisation consumed via APIs, event streams and batch; the UI unused. Entry-side ingest is largely there; exit-side maturity is not. It lands first as a Mode B deployment that progressively retires the UI: "Mode C is the destination, Mode B is the road."

Blue Box, Red Box, and the ICS score

Blue Box — standard multi-tenant, fast to provision, the default; product revenue, services bill near zero, two to four weeks to first opportunity. Red Box — dedicated single-tenant to the client's security spec; required for TPVP / ASRA clients; two to four months; every deal commits in writing to productising at least one capability back into Blue Box within two cycles. Easy Equities is the Red Box reference; MTN's fourteen-feed CRM build the canonical bespoke example. Deals classify on the Implementation Complexity Score (four dimensions, 0–4 each): 0–4 Blue Box · 5–8 Blue+ · 9–12 Red Box · 13–16 Red+ (board-level rationale or walk away). Auto-Red: air-gapped, on-prem, non-AWS with no prior deployment, heavy regulatory unknowns.

"Red box customers are the fuel for us to get to the blue box customers."Sangram — with the hub's caveat: "If we burn it and never reach the Blue Box, we are running an agency."

The honest tension: strategy points to Blue Box (velocity, compounding platform) but 80% of pipeline and existing revenue is Red Box (Sharifah), and today's POV-to-licence quality bar is a Red Box bar. Blue Box PMF is a hypothesis we are not yet measuring rigorously. Principle: Blue Box is scalable product revenue; Red Box is controlled strategic investment.

It rates complexity by ticket fidelity, dependencies, feature size and how much of the codebase is touched, then compares against historical work of similar complexity.
                      3. A human pass. A short sizing session per pod with the engineering lead, product facilitating, Albert present. The skill is the first pass that finds the gaps, not the answer.
                      4. A Bash Week adjustment. The historical data predates Bash Week, so raw output runs long. Albert reviews and cuts. Both numbers sit side by side in a sheet, one tab per pod: the raw estimate and the adjusted one.
                      5. Into the roadmap. Sized initiatives carry their estimate in the detail view, linked to that sheet. Milestone dates are then paced so the client bands beneath them still hold.

                      What that means for the dates

                      • The cut is judgement, not a measured multiplier. Nobody has a velocity figure from Bash Week yet. The adjustment is one experienced view of how much faster the new way of working is.
                      • Estimate quality tracks ticket quality. Where a ticket is thin the skill says so rather than guessing. Those items are the least reliable on the board.
                      • August capacity is not modelled. Most of engineering is in Spain, where August is holiday season. The skill counts working days without knowing who is away.
                      • Sequence is firmer than any single date. Milestones are anchored to what a client can do, not to a calendar promise. If a date moves, the order of the trenches is what holds.
                      • Q4-Q1 carries the contingency. Anything without a date sits there on purpose, and sits in Linear as Later with no date and no size.
